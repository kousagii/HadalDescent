7 CHAPTER 1 INTRODUCTION The ocean is one of the Earth's most important ecosystems, supporting diverse marine organisms and maintaining ecological balance. Despite its significance, public understanding of marine biodiversity remains an important concern, particularly regarding the characteristics, habitats, and ecological roles of marine species that inhabit deep-sea environments (National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration [NOAA], 2022; O'Brien et al., 2023). The limited accessibility of deep-sea ecosystems also presents challenges in providing opportunities for people to observe and understand marine organisms in their natural environments. International organizations continue to promote ocean literacy and education as important approaches to raising public awareness of marine ecosystems and supporting ocean conservation (McKinley et al., 2022). Traditional approaches to marine education, including textbooks, documentaries, and classroom instruction, provide valuable scientific information but may offer limited opportunities for learners to interact directly with marine environments. Game-based learning presents an alternative approach by incorporating exploration, challenges, and interactive activities into the learning process. Through these mechanics, learners can actively discover information, complete objectives, and receive feedback while engaging with educational content (Arboleya-García & Miralles, 2022; Pineda-Martínez et al., 2023). In particular, exploration-based games can provide virtual environments where players encounter and investigate organisms that may otherwise be difficult to observe in real life.

===== PDF PAGE 8 =====
8 To address this opportunity, this study proposes Hadal Descent: A 3D Underwater Exploration Game for Marine Biodiversity Awareness Using Perlin Noise-Based Procedural Content Generation and Context Steering Navigation . The project is an offline, single-player 3D mobile game designed primarily for senior high school and undergraduate students. Players control a research submarine and explore five ocean depth zones: Sunlight, Twilight, Midnight, Abyssal, and Hadal. Through marine species discovery, interactive scanning activities, environmental interaction, and an in-game Bestiary, players can access information about selected marine organisms, including their scientific names, characteristics, habitats, and interesting facts. The game integrates Procedural Content Generation (PCG) using Perlin Noise to create varied underwater terrain, combined with a rule-based ecological placement system that distributes marine organisms, habitats, debris, hazards, and resources according to predefined environmental and gameplay conditions. Context Steering is implemented to support the autonomous movement of marine organisms by allowing them to respond to surrounding obstacles and environmental conditions. Additionally, the Research Data Points (RDP) system rewards species scanning and environmental activities, allowing players to purchase submarine upgrades and meet the requirements for exploring deeper ocean zones. Developed using the Unity Game Engine and C#, with Blender for 3D asset creation and local storage for player progression, Hadal Descent combines underwater exploration, procedural environment generation, autonomous organism navigation, and introductory marine biodiversity education in an Android application. The study focuses on the development and evaluation of these integrated systems in terms of functionality, usability, educational content,

===== PDF PAGE 9 =====
9 gameplay experience, and technical performance. Through this approach, the project aims to provide an interactive platform for marine biodiversity awareness while examining the application of procedural generation and Context Steering in a mobile 3D exploration game. Background of the Study Marine biodiversity encompasses the variety of organisms, habitats, and ecological characteristics found in marine environments. Understanding this diversity is an important component of ocean literacy, which emphasizes the relationship between people and the ocean and the importance of informed decisions concerning marine environments (McKinley et al., 2022; UNESCO-IOC, 2024). However, public familiarity with marine organisms, their adaptations, and their ecological roles remains an area of concern (O'Brien et al., 2023). This challenge is particularly relevant to deep-sea biodiversity because many organisms inhabit environments that are difficult for the general public to access and observe. Consequently, alternative educational approaches are needed to provide accessible opportunities for learning about marine species and their habitats. The need for better participation in marine learning activities can be seen in a Philippine research. Avelino (2026) surveyed 383 undergraduate students in Bagac, Bataan and found that 86.9% had never participated in marine biodiversity studies, even though the respondents generally showed positive conservation values. It shows a gap between awareness and direct participation in marine-related activities. This finding supports exploring additional learning opportunities through activities that allow students to learn about marine organisms.

===== PDF PAGE 10 =====
10 Traditional learning resources, such as textbooks, classroom discussions, and documentaries, remain important for explaining marine biology. However, they do not always allow students to interact with marine environments or practice identifying organisms through exploration. Leitão et al. (2022) evaluated an ocean-literacy mobile application with secondary-school students and reported average correct-answer rates above 70% in the mobile-application conditions, compared with below 60% in the oral explanation and slideshow condition. Existing underwater games demonstrate different ways of incorporating marine environments into interactive experiences. Under the Waves emphasizes submarine exploration and environmental themes, while Loddlenaut incorporates ocean cleanup and interactions with marine creatures. Endless Ocean Luminous focuses on underwater exploration and marine species discovery, and FathomVerse uses interactive activities to introduce marine animal identification through underwater images (Quantic Dream, 2023; Moon Lagoon, 2023; Nintendo, 2024). These examples illustrate the potential of digital games to combine exploration, environmental interaction, and marine-related content. However, the present study focuses on integrating five ocean depth zones, procedurally generated underwater terrain, depth-based species distribution, interactive scanning, a Bestiary, and submarine progression within a single offline Android game. One technical problem in developing a 3D underwater exploration game is producing terrain that changes between exploration sessions while remaining suitable for mobile devices. Designing every seafloor manually can require additional development effort, while generating terrain through an algorithm introduces performance and design constraints. Ahmed and Pandey

===== PDF PAGE 11 =====
11 (2023) found that larger terrain grids increased Perlin Noise generation time and memory use. This suggests that terrain parameters must be carefully selected so that Hadal Descent can provide variation without creating excessive processing demands or blocking areas that players need to explore. Another problem is ensuring that procedurally generated environments still follow the intended characteristics of each ocean zone. Random placement alone may position marine species in unsuitable depths or habitats, which could present misleading educational information. For this reason, the game requires a rule- based ecological placement system supported by species-level references for habitat and depth ranges. Placement rules must be checked so that organisms are associated with appropriate environments rather than being distributed only for visual variety. The planned species database will use scientific references such as FishBase, SeaLifeBase, and the World Register of Marine Species (WoRMS), with records checked before final inclusion. In addition to environmental generation, autonomous movement is necessary to support interactions between marine organisms and the generated underwater terrain. Context Steering is a navigation approach that evaluates possible movement directions according to environmental information, including movement interests and potential dangers. Unlike systems that depend entirely on predetermined paths, it enables autonomous agents to adjust their movement in response to obstacles and surrounding conditions (Dockhorn et al., 2022). In Hadal Descent , Context Steering is used to support marine organism behaviors such as roaming and obstacle avoidance within the procedurally generated environments. Its implementation must account for

12 the game's different terrain layouts and environmental boundaries while remaining efficient enough for an Android-based application. Despite the existing use of game-based learning, marine exploration, and procedural generation in digital applications, there remains an opportunity to integrate these approaches into a single mobile game centered on progressive exploration of the five major ocean depth zones. The proposed game addresses this development gap by connecting environmental exploration with interactive species identification and structured learning content. Players must locate marine organisms, complete scanning mini-games, and unlock corresponding Bestiary entries containing introductory information about the discovered species. Successful scanning and other designated activities provide Research Data Points (RDP), which players can use to purchase submarine upgrades and satisfy the requirements for entering deeper zones. These interconnected mechanics are designed to encourage continued exploration while presenting marine biodiversity information within the gameplay experience. Accordingly, this study focuses on the development of Hadal Descent , an offline, single-player 3D underwater exploration game for Android. The project applies Perlin Noise-based Procedural Content Generation to terrain creation, rule-based placement to the distribution of marine organisms and environmental elements, and Context Steering to autonomous organism navigation. The resulting game is evaluated through functional and usability testing, assessment of its educational content and gameplay experience, and technical performance measurements. In particular, the study examines algorithmic performance using time, memory, adaptability, and variety as evaluation constraints, alongside sensitivity testing of selected algorithm parameters. Through these development and evaluation activities, the study

13 aims to establish how these techniques can be integrated into a mobile underwater exploration game intended to promote awareness of selected marine species and their habitats. Project Objectives The main objective of this project is to develop an underwater exploration game for marine biodiversity awareness that integrates a progression system, Procedural Content Generation (PCG), and Context Steering to deliver an engaging and educational gameplay experience while fostering awareness of marine biodiversity. Specifically, it aims to: 1. Develop an in-game Bestiary that supports marine literacy through factual species descriptions and 3D models. 2. Generate a dynamic 3D underwater environment using PCG, with Perlin Noise for terrain generation and a rule-based system for marine species and habitats across the five ocean depth zones. 3. Design a progression system that enables players to unlock succeeding ocean zones by completing exploration objectives and acquire Research Data Points (RDP) to purchase upgrades that improve navigation, exploration, and scanning efficiency. 4. Use Context Steering to make realistic navigation and autonomous movement behaviors for marine organisms such as roaming, obstacle avoidance, and interaction with the environment in procedurally generated undersea environments. 5. Enhance player awareness and understanding of marine biodiversity through interactive gameplay.

Scope and Delimitations SCOPE The proposed project will be developed as a standalone, offline, single-player 3D mobile application. The application will not require an active internet connection to function, as all game assets, player progression data, and bestiary will be stored locally on the user’s device. The application will be developed using the Unity Game Engine, with C# (C-Sharp) as the primary programming language. Blender will be used to create and optimize the 3D models of marine organisms, underwater environments, and other game assets.

===== PDF PAGE 16 =====
16 User Interface: The application will feature a unique and intuitive UI, including a number of functional screens. The main menu will allow the player to start a new game, load a game, go to the settings screen, and exit the game. In play, the Heads-Up Display (HUD) will display the zone of the ocean, the current number of Research Data Points (RDP), the sonar map, and shop and bestiary buttons. The Pause Menu will allow players to pause the game, change the sound settings, or return to the main menu. The Bestiary Screen will be an interactive encyclopedia of found species and silhouettes of undiscovered species. The final products will comprise educational data, including common name, scientific name, habitat, description and/or characteristics, and interesting facts about the species to promote awareness of marine biodiversity. The Upgrade Shop will enable players to buy submarine and research equipment upgrades. Gameplay: The focus of this game will be underwater exploration, identification of marine species, environmental awareness, and interaction/species scanning. The player will control a research submarine and explore five ocean depth zones: the Sunlight Zone (0–200 m), Twilight Zone (200–1,000 m), Midnight Zone (1,000–4,000 m), Abyssal Zone (4,000–6,000 m), and Hadal Zone (6,000–11,000+ m). Water depth and environmental conditions, geomorphic features, marine life, and habitats will be progressively deeper and different within each zone. Players will be able to explore and discover marine organisms, collect litter from the ocean, avoid danger, explore with the sonar system, and be able to perform the species scanning. Players will also pick up RDP, which can be used to uplevel their submarine's abilities and get them ready for deeper

===== PDF PAGE 17 =====
17 diving. There will be a tutorial at the beginning of the game, which will explain the basics of the game and the controls used. It will show players how to safely move the submarine, operate the sonar system, scan marine species, gather RDP, and navigate to the various zones of the ocean. The tutorial will be informative and give simple instructions before players engage in regular exploration. Ocean Zone and Species Distribution: There are 95 different marine species in total distributed over the five ocean zones, based on the intended depth range and habitat. There will be 15 species in the Sunlight Zone, 17 in the Twilight Zone, 19 in the Midnight Zone, 21 in the Abyssal Zone, and 23 in the Hadal Zone. The organisms chosen will be of varying types of marine life representative of the environments to be offered in the game. The Ocean Zone that a species belongs to will be used to determine where species can be placed in the generated underwater environments so that species only show up in habitats that allow them to in that Ocean Zone. The marine organisms that will be in the game will include a selection of marine biology, from that found in relatively shallow water down to the deep-sea-adapted organisms. Examples of species included in the project are clownfish (Amphiprion ocellaris), bigfin reef squid (Sepioteuthis lessoniana), blacktip reef shark (Carcharhinus melanopterus), blue-spotted ribbontail ray (Taeniura lymma), and other selected marine organisms. Deeper environments will feature life in deep-sea and hadal environments. The species information in the Bestiary will emphasize their identification, habitat, characteristics, and other basic information on marine biodiversity.

===== PDF PAGE 18 =====
18 The species distribution will help the player to be introduced to various marine species in the course of their progression in deeper waters. The species found in the upper parts of the levels will be species that are not suitable for shallower levels, and as the player moves over to the Abyssal and Hadal levels, the species found in the bottom will be species not found in shallower levels. Organisms that can be found in more than one depth range are dealt with as per the game's predefined distribution and are first found in the deeper zone and then in the shallower zone. Species Discovery and Scanning System: A species discovery and scanning system will feature in the application, enabling players to find, approach, and record marine life while exploring. Once a marine organism is encountered, the player will start the scanning process. There are two mini-games to scan that will be randomly selected for the game. In Capture & Focus, the player must follow the organism (which is moving) within the scanner's focus until the lock-on meter is full. In the second mini-game, the player must piece together the picture of the captured organism in a set amount of time (Reconstruction Scan). Counting the species will be recorded, and RDP will be awarded on successful completion of either mini-game, and the corresponding Bestiary information will be unlocked. If the player's attempt fails the scanning exercise, he/she will need to try again before the species can be successfully documented. Bestiary System: The application's main teaching element will be the Bestiary. When players complete the scanning and recording of the organisms, the species will be unlocked. Each finished entry will

===== PDF PAGE 19 =====
19 have information such as common name, scientific name, habitat, characteristics, and interesting facts about the organism. Species not yet observed will be depicted in silhouette and may serve as cues for other species to be explored. In this system, players collect marine species information as they move through each zone of the ocean. Player Progression: The player will be moving through the 5 ocean levels in order of depth. The player will start in the Sunlight Zone and will be required to explore the current zone, find and complete the required marine species list and meet the progression requirements before entering the next zone. The current game balance demands that the player find at least 50% of the species from the current zone, collect enough RDP to be able to upgrade the ship, and withstand the hull pressure to be able to progress to deeper zones. Once a zone is completed, it will stay accessible for the player to come back and explore other species not previously recorded. Players can thus keep on exploring finished zones and progress towards completing the whole bestiary and optional achievements. Saving and Loading System The application will have a saving and loading feature, which will enable the player to save the progress of the game locally on the device and load the game when the player chooses the Load Game option. Progress saved will contain the current ocean zone, ocean species discovered and recorded, RDP earned, and submarine upgrades purchased. This will allow players to continue their previous exploration without losing their progress. Research Data Point (RDP) System:

===== PDF PAGE 20 =====
20 The main in-game resource for progression is provided by the Research Data Point system. RDP will be collected mostly for successful scanning of marine species, for collecting ocean debris, and by completing selected optional marine educational activities. The accumulated RDP can be transferred to the Upgrade Shop to increase the submarine's movement speed, scanning equipment, sonar range, and navigation. assistance, battery life and hull pressure resistance. These will enable the player to descend to increasingly deeper depths in the ocean. Submarine Upgrade System: It will include an application where players can upgrade their research submarine's capabilities with RDP earned in exploration. Each upgrade system will have five tiers, offering increasing capabilities, including Hull, Sonar, Scanner, Engine, Light, and Utilities upgrades. Hull upgrades are primarily for progression, with each tier adding to the submarine's pressure resistance and enabling the player to enter deeper ocean zones. The upgrades to sonar improve its range of detection and give extra information about points of interest and marine life that help players navigate. Scanner upgrades enhance the focus bar in the scanning mini-games and offer extra perks in the Reconstruction Scan, like extra time and immediate tile-swap possibilities. Engine enhancements give the submarine more efficient movement and manoeuvrability, allowing for more efficient exploration and giving an extra movement advantage in some game situations. Light upgrades enhance submarine illumination and field of view, enabling players to better see and explore underwater locations, especially in the deeper and darker areas. The

===== PDF PAGE 21 =====
21 amount of RDP needed for each upgrade tier will vary depending on the tier; the higher the tier, the more RDP is required, as the player can explore more. Win/Completion Condition The game will be considered complete when the player successfully passes through all 5 ocean zones and reaches the deepest zone in the game, the Hadal Zone. The player needs to find at least half of the species found underwater in the zone and gather at least 50% of the Research Data Points (RDP) needed for the upgrades to the submarine, as well as the required hull pressure resistance, to progress to the next zone. Following the completion of the Hadal Zone, players can continue to explore the zones they have already completed and progress towards completing the bestiary and optional achievements. Procedural Content Generation (PCG): The application will generate different underwater environments in the five ocean zones with the help of procedural content generation. The ocean bottom, trenches, coral reefs, and other geologic features of the water will be created using Perlin Noise. Once the terrain has been created, a rule-based ecological placement system will be used to place marine species on the landscape, habitats, ocean debris, hazards, and ocean resources. Intended environmental conditions and habitat requirements of the various elements will be taken into consideration in the placement rules. This will minimize the need for individual design of each environment and enable different configurations of underwater elements in exploration. Context Steering Navigation:

===== PDF PAGE 22 =====
22 The application will use context steering to control the navigation and movement behavior of marine organisms in the procedurally generated underwater environments. Rather than calculating a fixed set of shortest paths, Context Steering continually checks several movements in various directions based on interest and danger information extracted from various environmental factors, including habitats, obstacles, and local danger. It allows marine life to travel naturally without having to cross terrains or environmental obstacles, stay within their ecological range, and search their habitats once they have strayed. Navigation decisions are continuously made, allowing context steering to be effective in procedurally generating underwater environments and to create robust, smooth, and realistic movement behavior for autonomous marine life. The educational aspect of the app will be centered on introducing the players to marine biodiversity and the features of selected marine organisms through interactive exploration and species documentation. The game will not be a strictly text-based learning experience, but it will enable players to find organisms in their marine environments, do some scanning, and retrieve educational information via the Bestiary. The educational content will be an awareness and knowledge of selected marine organisms and their habitats and is not intended to be a substitute for a formal marine biology course or academic program. DELIMITATIONS The study is delimited to the following: Game. Multiplayer, cooperative play, and online leaderboards are excluded. Cloud-based saving and syncing are not supported. Development is limited to mobile platforms (Android).

===== PDF PAGE 23 =====
23 Real-time hydrodynamic or fluid physics simulations are limited to what visual and gameplay presentation requires. Gameplay Systems. The game does not include infinite scaling and concludes once the Hadal Zone is fully explored. The Research Data Point (RDP) system is restricted to in-game progression, rewarding species discovery, debris collection, and optional learning activities; it does not simulate real-world currency, trading, or external economic systems. Submarine upgrades are limited to mobility, scanning, sonar, navigation, and pressure resistance attributes defined within the game’s systems. Algorithms. The terrain is generated using the Perlin Noise algorithm, and the marine species, habitats, ocean debris, hazards, and resource location distribution are based on a rule-based system, both of which are restricted to procedural content generation (PCG). It is not applicable to procedural narrative or dialogue generation. Context steering restricts the autonomous navigation and movement behaviour of marine organisms, such as roaming, obstacle avoidance, and interaction with the environment in procedurally generated underwater environments. These algorithms are designed for gameplay and educational purposes and do not seek to accurately model real-world oceanographic processes and/or marine ecosystem responses. Educational Scope. The Bestiary’s marine biology content is intended for introductory awareness and player engagement. It is not designed to replace formal academic curricula in marine biology. Marine Species Coverage. This game is based on a purposefully selected list of marine organisms, with 15 to 21 representative species selected for each of the five ocean zones. The lower end of this range is found in the deep oceans, including the Abyssal zone and the Hadal

===== PDF PAGE 24 =====
24 zone, because some taxonomic classes, e.g., cartilaginous fishes, marine mammals, and marine reptiles, do not live in this extreme environment. It does not attempt to model the full biodiversity of each depth level nor to model more detailed relationships like predator-prey, reproduction, migration, and population dynamics. Organisms were chosen for their unique morphological or ecological characteristics to maximize educational value and/or their visual recognizability. Species that occur across more than one zone of depth are first seen in the deepest possible zone, only to be seen in shallower zones after having been found in the Bestiary.

Game-based Learning Alotaibi (2024) conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis on game-based learning, evaluating its cognitive, emotional, and social impacts on learners. The review included 136 studies involving 1,426 participants and found that game-based learning produced positive effects on cognitive development, social development, emotional development, motivation, and engagement. The results indicate that game-based learning can support learning and development

26 by allowing learners to interact with educational content rather than relying only on passive instruction. Pineda-Martínez et al. Furthermore, incorporating educational content directly into gameplay mechanics helps students better understand complex subjects while promoting educational inclusion and developing collaborative competencies Arboleya-García and Miralles (2022) developed and evaluated an interdisciplinary educational game designed to enhance environmental awareness and marine literacy among primary and secondary students. The authors reported that embedding domain-specific tasks directly into structured game mechanics arouses student interest, enhances intrinsic motivation, and aids in the retention of scientific concepts. By connecting learning objectives with playful problem-solving, the study demonstrated that educational games effectively bridge the gap between abstract biological principles and practical understanding. Leitão et al. (2022) examined the use of a gamified mobile application to promote ocean literacy among 98 secondary school students aged 11 to 14. The application incorporated game elements such as points, badges, leaderboards, and interactive activities related to ocean-related issues. Students using the mobile application achieved more than 70% correct answers, compared with less than 60% among students who received oral explanations and slideshow presentations. The use of points, badges, and problem-based learning also resulted in an

===== PDF PAGE 27 =====
27 improvement of approximately 30% in learning outcomes compared with the control condition. The findings support the use of mobile game-based learning as an interactive approach for communicating environmental concepts and encouraging environmental awareness. Georgiou et al. However, the evidence for behavioral change was less consistent, with 38.6% reporting positive effects and other studies reporting mixed or no effects. These findings suggest that environmental games can be particularly useful for improving knowledge and attitudes, although changes in actual behavior require further investigation. These findings are particularly relevant to mobile-based marine education because mobile games can provide learners with accessible and interactive environments for exploring scientific concepts. Marine Biodiversity Awareness and Ocean Literacy Marine biodiversity awareness is knowledge about the diversity of marine organisms, ecosystems, habitats, and their ecological importance. Awareness of marine biodiversity is essential because knowledge of marine life and ecosystems can shape people's understanding and responses to marine conservation challenges. Avelino (2026) investigated marine biodiversity awareness among 383 undergraduate students in the Philippines. The study found that although students demonstrated strong pro-conservation values, 86.9% had never participated in marine research. The findings emphasize the need to provide students with more opportunities to engage with marine biodiversity and local conservation efforts. Ocean literacy is not just about knowledge of the various organisms in the ocean, but also about people's understanding of the human-ocean connection. Asikin et al. (2025) defined ocean literacy as knowledge of marine ecosystems, environmental issues, and the significance of marine conservation. Their systematic review identified approaches such as project-based learning, problem-based learning, and inquiry-based learning as methods used to develop ocean literacy. Game-based learning and virtual reality were also identified as technology-supported approaches that can provide interactive learning experiences involving marine biodiversity, conservation, and ocean sustainability. Bastos et al. (2026) conducted a systematic scoping review on ocean literacy education and reviewed 122 studies on ocean learning in formal education. Their results revealed that

===== PDF PAGE 29 =====
29 ocean education is a growing field of research and that many of the current learning experiences are rather limited and tend to focus on the ocean as a physical place. The authors stressed the importance of wider and multidisciplinary learning opportunities to take into account the ecological, social, and human aspects of the ocean. This means that ocean literacy education does not simply need to convey factual knowledge about the ocean but should also teach students the broader importance of marine ecosystems and the human connection to the ocean. The need for accessible marine education is especially relevant to the Philippines. The country has rich marine biodiversity, but Avelino (2026) identified a gap between students' pro-conservation values and their actual participation in marine research. The purpose of each layer is clearly defined and each plays its role to enable the exploration, learning, and progression of the game. It allows the

===== PDF PAGE 47 =====
47 system components to communicate with each other in an organized way by separating the user interface, game logic, and data. It displays everything from the Main Menu to the HUD, the Bestiary, the Upgrade Shop, the scanning interface, and the 3D models, images, and sounds of the game. The Player actions, including movement of the submarine, species scanning, opening the Bestiary, and buying submarine upgrades, are passed to the Logic/Application Layer for processing. The results, including the discovery of new species, upgrades, and zone progressions, are then presented to the player. The Logic/Application Layer is the heart of the system and the mechanism of how the game plays and is implemented by its major systems such as species scanning, exploration, Bestiary progression, RDP, submarine upgrades, Context Steering, and PCG. PCG generates underwater terrain by using Perlin Noise, and species, habitats, debris, hazards, and resources are placed using rule-based placement. By assessing habitats, obstacles, and environmental hazards, Context Steering regulates the movement of marine organisms. The input from players, the logic of the game, and communication with the Presentation and Data Layers are handled by this layer. The Data Layer is responsible for maintaining and accessing the data that the game needs. Progress in Hadal Descent is tracked locally on the device, and it is an offline, single-player mobile application, so progress, Bestiary, and other required information are stored locally on the device. That includes player progression, discovered species, RDP, submarine upgrades, ocean zone progression, and game configuration. The layer acts as an interface for the Logic/Application Layer to pass data to it as needed, and will store updated data to enable players to save their progress, upgrades, and discovered Bestiary entries between game sessions.

Since Hadal Descent is designed as an offline mobile game, memory efficiency is important to maintain stable performance across Android devices. Lower memory consumption allows the game to support more assets, species, and environment features without degrading performance. Greater variety contributes to replayability and exploration-based learning experiences. Design Trade-offs (Comparative Analysis) Perlin Noise is a gradient noise algorithm commonly used in Procedural Content Generation (PCG) to create natural-looking terrain. Its primary strength is its ability to generate smooth and continuous environmental features while requiring relatively low computational resources compared to manually designed maps. Furthermore, Perlin Noise can create varied terrain through adjustments in frequency, amplitude, and seed values. However, it may produce repetitive patterns when parameters are not carefully configured, and increasing terrain complexity may increase computational requirements. Context Steering is an autonomous navigation technique that evaluates multiple movement directions through interest and danger maps. Its main strength is adaptability, as marine organisms can react dynamically to obstacles and environmental conditions without requiring predefined navigation paths. This makes it suitable for procedurally generated environments where navigation meshes may be impractical. However, increasing the number of steering rays or detection distances may increase computational overhead because more environmental information must be processed continuously. The Rule-Based Placement System distributes marine species, habitats, debris, hazards, and resources according to predefined ecological rules. Its strengths include predictable

===== PDF PAGE 97 =====
97 ecological placement and low computational complexity. It ensures that species appear in appropriate ocean zones and habitat conditions. However, because placement follows predefined rules, the system may produce less variation compared to fully stochastic generation approaches if the rules are overly restrictive. 102 The implemented Context Steering system evaluates multiple directional probes to determine suitable movement directions. It uses an Interest Map to identify directions toward a target and a Danger Map to detect nearby obstacles and terrain boundaries. These directions are evaluated using spherecasts to detect obstacles and determine the available clearance for marine organisms. The steering score for each direction is calculated by multiplying the interest value by the square of the remaining clearance, represented by the equation 2 𝑆 = 𝐼 ( 1 − 𝐷 ) where S is the steering score for direction, I is the interest value representing the direction's alignment toward the target and D is the danger value representing the proximity of obstacles or environmental boundaries. The Interest Map assigns higher values to directions aligned with the target, while the Danger Map assigns higher values to directions with nearby obstacles. The equation reduces the steering score as danger increases, with the quadratic clearance factor providing a stronger reduction for directions approaching obstacles. This allows marine organisms to prioritize safer directions while maintaining movement toward their targets. The system then uses the resulting steering direction to control the movement and rotation of marine organisms. Parameters that will be analyzed are the number of steering rays and the distance of the obstacle detection radius, and the speed of moving and the speed of turning will be constant for testing. 107 Parameters and Configurations The parameters and configurations specify the values and conditions that are used by the algorithms implemented in Hadal Descent. The procedural generation system relies on a Perlin Noise algorithm for generating underwater terrain and a rules-based system for placement of marine species, habitats, debris, hazards, and resources. Autonomous motion of marine organisms in the generated environment is controlled by using context steering. The parameters are set as per the needs of each system and ocean zone. Perlin Noise. The five ocean zones have underwater terrain generated using Perlin Noise. The terrain generation is based on a 96 × 96 resolution and has zone-specific values for noise frequency, amplitude, and seed. Multiple noise layers are used to produce variations in the terrain, while biome thresholds are used to determine different terrain characteristics. The frequency dictates the size and frequency of terrain features, the seed determines the variety of terrains, and the amplitude determines how much the noise affects the terrain height. Rule-Based Placement. The rule-based system is used to assign elements of the environment based on a set of rules. The environment props are set with a cluster of 40 props, 16 props per cluster, and a cluster radius of 6.5 m; coral is set with a minimum spacing of 0.9 m. The placement of marine species is based on factors like preferred biome, minimum and maximum depth range, terrain and slope requirements, and a minimum separation of 5 meters between instances. There are also a maximum of 60 spawn attempts in the species spawning system to search for suitable spawn sites. The default for debris placement is that it is configured

===== PDF PAGE 108 =====
108 with 4 debris clusters per zone. The rules are used to assure the distribution of generated elements based on conditions set for each of the ocean zones. Context Steering. Context Steering: autonomous movement of marine organisms in the generated environment. The movement system uses 12 steering rays and a 5-meter obstacle detection range to detect surrounding terrain and obstacles. The movement speed is determined by the species configuration, while the turn speed is set to 3.5. Avoidance of areas and preferred movement directions are based on interest and danger. Vertical movement and zone depth boundaries are also taken into account in the system to facilitate the appropriate movement within the designated ocean zone. The configurations are employed depending on the needs of the various ocean zones and help to realize procedural terrain creation, rule-based environment placement, exploration, and the evolution of autonomous marine organisms. The parameters will also provide a foundation for testing and experimental procedures to test the implemented systems. The Perlin Noise-based terrain generation system and Context Steering system will be evaluated, and the rule-based system will be observed to ensure species, habitats, debris, hazards and resources are placed correctly in the created environment.
